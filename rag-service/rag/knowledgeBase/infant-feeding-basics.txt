Title: Infant Feeding Basics by Age
Topic: infant, feeding

0 to 6 months:
Most guidance recommends breast milk or formula as the sole source of
nutrition during this period, feeding on a responsive schedule (following the
baby's hunger and fullness cues rather than a strict clock). Newborns often
feed 8-12 times per day; frequency naturally spaces out over the following
months.

Around 6 months:
Most babies show readiness for solid foods around this age — signs include
being able to sit with support, good head control, and showing interest in
food. Solids are typically introduced alongside continued breast milk or
formula, not as a replacement, starting with simple, iron-rich foods
(fortified cereals, pureed meats, legumes) and single-ingredient purees to
watch for reactions.

6 to 12 months:
Texture and variety typically increase gradually — from purees to mashed and
soft finger foods — while breast milk or formula remains the main source of
nutrition and calories through the first year.

General notes:
- Introduce common allergens (peanut, egg, dairy) early and one at a time per
  current guidance, rather than delaying them, unless a doctor advises
  otherwise for a specific baby.
- Avoid honey before 12 months due to infant botulism risk.
- Avoid cow's milk as a primary drink before 12 months.

This is general guidance; a pediatrician can tailor feeding advice to an
individual baby's growth and needs.
