Title: Understanding M-CHAT Autism Screening
Topic: mchat, autism, screening

M-CHAT-style questionnaires are screening tools, commonly used around 16-30
months, designed to help identify toddlers who might benefit from a more
thorough developmental evaluation. They are not diagnostic tests — a screening
result on its own cannot confirm or rule out autism.

How results are generally interpreted:
- A low-risk score suggests the child is showing expected social and
  communication behaviors for their age based on the questions asked, though
  ongoing routine developmental monitoring by a pediatrician still matters.
- A medium-risk score generally means some follow-up questions or a repeat
  screening after a short interval is recommended, since some initial
  "risk" answers turn out not to reflect a real concern.
- A high-risk score generally means a referral for a full developmental
  evaluation is recommended, ideally without a long delay, so that any
  support needed can start as early as possible if warranted.

Important context for parents:
- A single flagged item does not mean a diagnosis is likely — many
  neurotypical toddlers show one or two atypical answers on any given day.
- Early evaluation is generally recommended when risk is elevated because
  early support tends to help regardless of the eventual diagnosis, not
  because a positive screen means autism is confirmed.
- Screening results can be affected by a child's mood, tiredness, or
  unfamiliarity with the questions on a given day; a pattern over time is more
  informative than a single screening.

This is general educational information. A pediatrician or developmental
specialist should always interpret an individual child's specific results.
