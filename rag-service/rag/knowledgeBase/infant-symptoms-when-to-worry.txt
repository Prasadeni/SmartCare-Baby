Title: Common Infant Symptoms and When They Need a Doctor
Topic: infant, symptoms

Babies get minor coughs, rashes, and unsettled days often, and most of the time
these resolve on their own. Knowing which symptoms are usually mild versus
which need prompt medical attention can help parents decide what to do.

Generally lower urgency (monitor, mention at next visit if it persists):
- Mild, occasional spit-up without distress
- A single mild rash with no fever, feeding well, acting normally
- Mild, intermittent cough without breathing difficulty
- Slightly reduced appetite for less than a day, otherwise alert and active

Generally warrants contacting a doctor same-day:
- Fever in a baby (see the separate fever guidance for age-specific thresholds)
- Persistent vomiting, especially if unable to keep fluids down
- Diarrhea lasting more than a day or two, or with signs of dehydration
  (fewer wet diapers, dry mouth, unusually sleepy)
- A rash that is spreading quickly, looks like bruising, or doesn't fade when
  pressed
- Noticeably fussier or more lethargic than usual for more than a few hours

Generally an emergency (seek immediate care):
- Difficulty breathing, rapid or labored breathing, or blue-tinged lips/skin
- Unresponsive, extremely difficult to wake, or limp
- A seizure
- A fever in a very young infant (see fever guidance — young infants need
  faster evaluation)
- Signs of severe dehydration (no wet diaper for many hours, sunken soft spot,
  no tears when crying)

When in doubt, it's always reasonable to call a pediatrician's after-hours
line or a nurse advice line to ask — that's what they're there for. This
information is general and doesn't replace an individual medical evaluation.
