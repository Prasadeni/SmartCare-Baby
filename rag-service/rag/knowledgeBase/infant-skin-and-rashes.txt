Title: Infant Skin and Rash Concerns
Topic: infant, symptoms, skin

Newborn and infant skin goes through a lot of harmless changes, but a few
rash patterns are worth knowing how to tell apart.

Usually harmless, common in the early weeks:
- Newborn acne: small red or white bumps on the face, typically resolving
  within a few weeks without treatment
- Milia: tiny white bumps, usually on the nose or face, resolving on their own
- Cradle cap: yellowish, scaly patches on the scalp; usually harmless and
  often improves with gentle washing
- Heat rash: small red bumps in areas prone to sweating, easing when the baby
  is cooled down and dressed lightly

Worth mentioning to a doctor, not urgent:
- A persistent rash lasting more than a couple of weeks despite basic care
- A rash accompanied by mild itching that seems to bother the baby
- Dry, scaly patches suggestive of eczema, especially with a family history

Needs prompt or emergency evaluation:
- A rash that doesn't fade when you press a glass or finger against it
  (can indicate a serious infection)
- A rash spreading rapidly, especially with fever
- Widespread blistering or peeling skin
- A rash that looks like bruising and appeared without a clear cause
- Any rash accompanied by difficulty breathing, facial swelling, or signs of
  an allergic reaction

This is general information; a pediatrician can examine a specific rash and
give guidance tailored to that baby.
