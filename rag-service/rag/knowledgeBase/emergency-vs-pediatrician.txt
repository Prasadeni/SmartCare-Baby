Title: When to Call Emergency Services vs. a Pediatrician
Topic: emergency, guidance

Knowing which option to use can save critical time in a genuine emergency,
while avoiding unnecessary emergency room visits for things a pediatrician's
office can handle.

Call emergency services / go to the emergency room for:
- Difficulty breathing, choking, or blue-tinged lips or skin
- Unresponsiveness or extreme difficulty waking
- A seizure, especially a first-time one
- A serious injury, significant fall, or suspected broken bone
- Severe allergic reaction (swelling of face/throat, sudden widespread rash
  with breathing difficulty)
- Heavy, uncontrolled bleeding
- A fever in a baby under 3 months old

Call or message the pediatrician (same-day or next available) for:
- Fever in an older baby who is otherwise alert and feeding
- A rash without other severe symptoms
- Ongoing vomiting or diarrhea without signs of severe dehydration
- Feeding difficulties or noticeable behavior changes lasting more than a day
- Questions about medication dosing or minor injuries

If uncertain which category a situation falls into, many pediatric practices
and health systems offer a 24-hour nurse advice line — calling that line is a
reasonable middle step when something feels concerning but doesn't clearly
look like a 911/emergency-room situation.
