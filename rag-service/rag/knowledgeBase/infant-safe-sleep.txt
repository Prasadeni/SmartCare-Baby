Title: Safe Sleep Guidelines for Infants
Topic: infant, sleep, safety

Safe sleep practices are widely recommended to reduce the risk of Sudden
Infant Death Syndrome (SIDS) and other sleep-related infant deaths.

Core recommendations, generally through the first year:
- Always place a baby on their back to sleep, for naps and at night
- Use a firm, flat sleep surface (a crib or bassinet meeting current safety
  standards) with a fitted sheet only
- Keep the sleep area clear of pillows, loose blankets, bumpers, and soft toys
- Room-sharing (baby's own sleep surface in the parents' room) is generally
  recommended for at least the first several months, without bed-sharing
- Avoid overheating; dress the baby lightly rather than heavily bundling
- Offering a pacifier at sleep times has been associated with reduced SIDS
  risk in some guidance
- Avoid smoke exposure, both during pregnancy and after birth

If a baby falls asleep somewhere other than a safe sleep surface (a car seat,
swing, or someone's arms), it's generally recommended to move them to a safe,
flat surface as soon as it's practical to do so.

This is general safety information; a pediatrician can address specific
questions about an individual baby's sleep setup.
