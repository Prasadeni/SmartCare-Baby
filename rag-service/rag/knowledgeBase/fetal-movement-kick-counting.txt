Title: Fetal Movement and Kick Counting
Topic: pregnancy, kick-counter

Feeling regular movement from your baby is one of the most reassuring signs of
wellbeing in the second half of pregnancy. Most people start noticing consistent
kicks between 18 and 25 weeks, and movement patterns typically become more
regular by the third trimester.

There's no single "correct" number of kicks everyone should feel — babies have
their own patterns, and movement is usually strongest after meals, in the
evening, or when you're lying down and paying attention. A common approach to
kick counting is timing how long it takes to feel 10 distinct movements; many
providers suggest this shouldn't typically take more than two hours once you've
started counting.

What matters more than an exact number is a noticeable change in your baby's
usual pattern — for example, if movement that used to happen every day suddenly
becomes much less frequent, weaker, or stops. A single quiet day is not
automatically alarming, especially if the baby has been very active previously,
but a clear, sustained drop from what's normal for that pregnancy is something
to have checked rather than wait out.

When to contact a provider promptly:
- A significant decrease in movement compared to your baby's usual pattern
- No movement felt over a period that feels unusual for your baby
- Movement is felt but seems noticeably weaker over more than a day

This information is general and not a substitute for guidance from your own
obstetric provider, who can advise on what's appropriate for your specific
pregnancy.
