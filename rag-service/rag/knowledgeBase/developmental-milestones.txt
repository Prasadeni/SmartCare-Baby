Title: Developmental Milestones by Age
Topic: milestones, development

Milestones are general guideposts, not strict deadlines — babies vary, and a
range of a few weeks or months around these markers is typically normal.

Around 2 months:
- Motor: lifts head briefly during tummy time
- Social: smiles responsively at people
- Language: makes cooing sounds

Around 4 months:
- Motor: holds head steady, may roll from tummy to back
- Social: smiles spontaneously, especially at people
- Language: babbles, laughs

Around 6 months:
- Motor: rolls both directions, sits briefly with support
- Cognitive: reaches for and brings objects to mouth
- Social: recognizes familiar faces, responds to own name

Around 9 months:
- Motor: sits without support, may pull to stand
- Cognitive: looks for objects that are hidden while watching
- Language: babbles with consonant sounds (mama, dada, non-specifically)
- Social: shows stranger wariness in some babies

Around 12 months:
- Motor: pulls to stand, may take first independent steps
- Cognitive: explores objects by shaking, banging, dropping
- Language: says one or two words with meaning, responds to simple requests
- Social: plays simple interactive games (peekaboo), points to show interest

Around 18 months:
- Motor: walks well, may run stiffly
- Language: says several words, points to body parts when asked
- Social: imitates others, shows affection toward familiar people

A single delayed milestone is not automatically a cause for concern, but a
pattern of multiple delays, or loss of a previously acquired skill, is worth
discussing with a pediatrician.
