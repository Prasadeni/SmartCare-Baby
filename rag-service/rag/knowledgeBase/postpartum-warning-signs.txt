Title: Postpartum Warning Signs for the Mother
Topic: postpartum, mother, emergency

The weeks after birth are a period where a mother's body is recovering
significantly, and while most recoveries go smoothly, some symptoms warrant
prompt medical attention.

Seek urgent or emergency care for:
- Heavy vaginal bleeding (soaking through a pad in an hour or less) or
  passing large clots
- Severe headache that doesn't improve, especially with vision changes or
  swelling — can signal postpartum preeclampsia
- Fever, chills, or a foul-smelling vaginal discharge — can signal infection
- Chest pain, difficulty breathing, or a leg that's suddenly swollen, red, or
  painful — can signal a blood clot
- A C-section or perineal incision that's increasingly red, swollen, painful,
  or draining pus
- Overwhelming thoughts of harming yourself or the baby, or feeling unable to
  care for yourself or the baby — this needs immediate support, not just
  routine follow-up

Emotional wellbeing:
Mood changes ("baby blues") are common in the first couple of weeks and
usually ease on their own. Symptoms that are severe, don't improve after two
weeks, or include thoughts of self-harm are different from typical baby blues
and deserve prompt attention from a healthcare provider — postpartum
depression and anxiety are common and very treatable.

This is general information, not a diagnosis. A treating provider should
evaluate any specific postpartum symptoms.
