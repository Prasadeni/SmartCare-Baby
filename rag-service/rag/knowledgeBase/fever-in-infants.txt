Title: Fever in Infants — When It's an Emergency
Topic: infant, symptoms, fever

Fever is generally defined as a temperature of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher,
measured rectally for the most accuracy in young infants. How urgently a
fever needs evaluation depends heavily on the baby's age.

Under 3 months old:
Any fever of 38°C (100.4°F) or higher in a baby under 3 months is generally
treated as needing prompt medical evaluation, often the same day or via
emergency care, because young infants can become seriously unwell quickly and
are harder to assess just by how they look or act.

3 to 6 months old:
A fever in this range still generally warrants a same-day call to the
pediatrician, especially if the baby seems unwell, is feeding poorly, or the
fever is high (39°C / 102.2°F or above).

Over 6 months old:
A fever alone in an older, otherwise alert baby who is feeding and behaving
close to normal is often manageable at home with guidance from a pediatrician
on comfort measures, but should still be discussed with a doctor if it lasts
more than a day or two, is very high, or is accompanied by other concerning
symptoms (rash, breathing difficulty, unusual sleepiness, refusing fluids).

Seek immediate/emergency care regardless of age if a fever comes with:
- Difficulty breathing
- A rash that doesn't fade when pressed, or looks like bruising
- Unusual sleepiness, difficulty waking, or limpness
- A seizure
- Signs of dehydration

This is general guidance, not a substitute for a pediatrician's assessment of
an individual baby.
